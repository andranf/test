On Autonomy: What It Actually Means to Run a Course in Australia
Andrew
19 March 2026
7 min read

Australian greenkeeping has always demanded a certain self-reliance. We're geographically isolated, understaffed relative to counterparts overseas, and expected to deliver world-class surfaces on tight budgets. But I think there's something deeper going on with autonomy in this industry, and it's worth unpacking.

The Word Gets Used Loosely

When I say autonomy, I'm not talking about going rogue or ignoring agronomic science. I mean something more specific: the capacity to make confident, well-reasoned decisions without constantly seeking external validation — from consultants, from chemical reps, from what other courses are doing.

It's a disposition, not a technique.

Why Australia Demands It More

In the UK or the US, you can have a bad season and your regional agronomist is a two-hour drive away. In Australia, you might be in a situation where the nearest experienced colleague is interstate, the product registrations are different, and the climate swings in ways that no overseas publication prepared you for.

What It Looks Like in Practice

  • ▸Knowing your environment deeply. Not textbook soil science, but your soil. How it behaves in February versus August.
  • ▸Understanding mechanisms, not just recipes. If you know why a nitrogen application does what it does, you can adapt when the product you usually use isn't available.
  • ▸Data that you actually trust. Affordable sensors, better weather data, disease forecasting models — helping greenkeepers develop a data-backed intuition.
  • ▸A willingness to be wrong. Autonomy without humility is just stubbornness.

Where I Land

I think autonomy in Australian greenkeeping is both a practical necessity and a professional ideal worth pursuing deliberately. Not because external expertise is bad — it isn't — but because the people who do this job best are the ones who use that expertise to sharpen their own thinking, not replace it.

This blog is partly about working through what that looks like. More to come.
